# Evidence on Predictors of Big-Winner (Multi-Bagger) Stocks: Academic Factors, Practitioner Studies, and Counter-Evidence

Scope note: Nearly all evidence below is from US data (CRSP/Compustat, NYSE/AMEX/Nasdaq) unless stated. Search snippets and secondary summaries were used where primary PDFs were not readable. Each claim says where it comes from. Many effect sizes come from secondary summaries (Alpha Architect, Swedroe/ETF.com, Quantpedia, The Idea Farm, UCLA Anderson Review), and they are labelled as such.

## Q1. Which measurable signals have the strongest, most replicated evidence for predicting future large outperformance?

### Takeaway
The best-replicated signals for the cross-section of returns are intermediate-horizon price momentum, nearness to the 52-week high, earnings momentum (SUE/earnings-announcement return, analyst revisions), gross profitability, and the high-volume return premium. All of these predict *average* returns over months, typically worth a few percent a year in long-short spreads. None of them reliably identifies which individual stocks will become multi-baggers. Bessembinder's direct test of long-horizon extreme winners finds that observable characteristics explain under about 2% of the cross-section of decade returns. Several signals, notably PEAD in large caps, have decayed sharply since the mid-2000s.

### Cited Findings

**Price momentum (Jegadeesh & Titman 1993; Asness et al.)**
- Jegadeesh & Titman (1993), "Returns to Buying Winners and Selling Losers," JF. Per Swedroe's summary, the momentum premium was 10.9%/yr over 1927–1993 and roughly halved to 5.5%/yr post-publication (1994–2015). That is about 0.9%/month in-sample. — [Swedroe, ETF.com "Is Momentum Really Dead?"](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-momentum-really-dead)
- Swedroe also reports the Fama-French UMD factor premium for 2010–2015 at 6.8%/yr, positive in 5 of 6 years, with one calendar-year loss of about 1%. — [Swedroe, ETF.com](https://www.etf.com/sections/index-investor-corner/swedroe-momentum-really-dead)
- Asness, Frazzini, Israel & Moskowitz, "Fact, Fiction, and Momentum Investing" (JPM 2014). They cite data back to 1801, more than 20 years of out-of-sample evidence, and 40+ countries. One summary reports momentum at 6.3%/yr vs value at 3.6%/yr (1991–2013). The paper concedes the prior decade's UMD return was around the 7th percentile of rolling 10-year windows since 1927. It recommends combining momentum with value to cut drawdowns. — [AQR PDF](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/JPM-Fact-Fiction-and-Momentum-Investing.pdf)
- Chan, Jegadeesh & Lakonishok (1996), "Momentum Strategies," JF 51(5):1681–1713 (NYSE/AMEX/Nasdaq, 1977–1993). Past returns and past earnings surprise *each* predict drift after controlling for the other. Analysts' forecasts respond sluggishly to news. Size and book-to-market do not explain the drifts. There is little evidence of later reversal for high price- and earnings-momentum stocks. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v51y1996i5p1681-1713.html); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/quantitative-momentum-research-price-and-earnings-momentum/)
- The 1999 FAJ follow-up by the same authors says the strategies are profitable at intermediate horizons but carry high turnover, so trading costs must be managed. — [CFA Institute](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/1999/the-profitability-of-momentum-strategies)
- Novy-Marx (2015) argues that once you control for underreaction to earnings surprises, price momentum is no longer a useful stock-selection signal. In his view, earnings momentum subsumes price momentum. — [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/quantitative-momentum-research-price-and-earnings-momentum/)

**52-week-high proximity (George & Hwang 2004, JF)**
- Nearness to the 52-week high "dominates and improves upon" past returns in forecasting future returns. Its forecasts do not reverse in the long run, which suggests short-term momentum and long-term reversal are separate phenomena. — [George & Hwang 2004 summary (SUFE academic newsletter)](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/361244)
- A thesis summarising the paper reports US abnormal returns of about 0.45%/month for long-near-high / short-far-from-high. Out of sample: Australia about 2.14%/month (2005 study), positive in 17 of 18 markets (12 significant), and better than traditional momentum across 20 countries (Liu et al. 2011). — [Massey University thesis](https://mro.massey.ac.nz/handle/10179/891?show=full)
- George, Hwang & Li link the 52-week-high effect to the q-factor model. Nearness to the 52-week high predicts future ROE and investment growth, not just returns. — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/52-week-high-q-factor-investment-model/)

**Post-earnings-announcement drift (PEAD) / SUE / earnings-announcement returns ("gaps")**
- Brandt, Kishore, Santa-Clara & Venkatachalam, "Earnings Announcements are Full of Surprises" (2008). They sort on the 3-day abnormal return around the announcement (EAR), which effectively captures the earnings "gap". One summary reports EAR-sorted portfolios earn 7.55%/yr abnormal, 1.3 pp more than SUE. A Quantpedia page reports about 12.5%/yr for a combined top-SUE ∩ top-EAR long-short held one quarter. The two figures conflict and the original was not verified. — [Quantpedia PEAD](https://quantpedia.com/strategies/post-earnings-announcement-effect/)
- A KTH thesis contradicts the EAR > SUE ranking and finds SUE drift larger. — [KTH thesis](https://www.math.kth.se/matstat/seminarier/reports/M-exjobb16/160601c.pdf)
- **Decay:** Martineau, "Rest in Peace Post-Earnings Announcement Drift" (Critical Finance Review 2022). PEAD in non-microcap US stocks began fading in 2001 and had "completely disappeared" by 2006. Prices now absorb the surprise on announcement day. He attributes this to decimalisation and post-2005 HFT. Pre-announcement drift also weakened. — [Martineau PDF](https://cfr.ivo-welch.info/published/papers/martineau2021rest.pdf); [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again/)
- **Counter-claim:** Dickerson, Julliard & Mueller (JFE, in press) and Hirshleifer, Peng & Wang (RFS 2025, drift t ≈ 14) find PEAD persists, but neither excludes microcaps. Subrahmanyam (2025 WP, Feb 2001–Dec 2024) gets t = 2.18 with all stocks and t = 1.43 (insignificant) excluding microcaps (bottom 20% NYSE cap, about 3% of market value). Surviving PEAD is therefore a microcap phenomenon. — [UCLA Anderson Review](https://anderson-review.ucla.edu/is-post-earnings-announcement-drift-a-thing-again/)
- A 2026 paper argues the PEAD decline mainly reflects current earnings becoming less predictive of future earnings, not more arbitrage. Only the abstract was available. — [Sage journal abstract](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0148558X261439734)

**Analyst estimate revisions**
- Chan, Jegadeesh & Lakonishok (1996) use analyst forecast revisions as one of three earnings-momentum measures, alongside SUE and announcement returns. All three predict drift, and analysts underreact, especially for the worst past performers. — [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/quantitative-momentum-research-price-and-earnings-momentum/); [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v51y1996i5p1681-1713.html)

**Profitability / quality (Novy-Marx 2013, JFE 108(1):1–28)**
- Gross profits-to-assets predicts the cross-section of returns about as well as book-to-market. Profitable firms earn significantly higher returns despite higher valuation ratios. Profitability strategies are "growth" strategies that hedge value. Controlling for profitability "dramatically increases" value-strategy performance, especially among large, liquid stocks. — [NBER w15940](https://nber.org/papers/w15940)
- Secondary figure: a combined profitability + value strategy over 1963–2010 shows about 7.7%/yr with 9.8% volatility (Quantpedia figure, not the paper's own spread). — [Quantpedia via search summary](https://vvv.quantpedia.com/?p=531)

**High-volume return premium and volume evidence**
- Gervais, Kaniel & Mingelgrin (2001), JF 56(3):877–919. Stocks with unusually high (low) volume over a day or week tend to rise (fall) over the following month. The authors attribute this to a visibility/attention shock. Return autocorrelation, firm announcements, market risk and liquidity do not explain it. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v56y2001i3p877-919.html); [Wharton WP PDF](https://rodneywhitecenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/9901.pdf)
- Kaniel, Li & Starks find the premium in almost all developed markets and in emerging markets. — [Duke Scholars / search summary](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/773086)

**Direct tests on extreme long-horizon winners (Bessembinder, "Extreme Stock Market Performers" I–IV, 2020)**
- US data. The decade-ranking analysis uses 1950–2019 and the best/worst 200 stocks each decade. Top-decade performers had beforehand: higher R&D (about 4.8% of assets vs 2.8% average), much more equity issuance (about 26% of assets vs 9%), large prior-decade drawdowns (about 81%), and younger age. Bottom performers had higher leverage (about 57.6%), higher volatility, and negative profitability (income/assets about −10%). — [The Idea Farm, Part IV summary](https://theideafarm.com/markets/extreme-stock-market-performers-part-iv-can-observable-characteristics-forecast-outcomes/); [Klement, Evidence Investor](https://www.evidenceinvestor.com/post/how-hard-is-it-to-find-the-next-amazon)
- **Key result:** these characteristics explain only about 2% of the cross-section of subsequent decade returns (>98% unexplained). Bessembinder concludes investors cannot systematically identify future outliers. One summary of Part I gives 7.6% explained instead, so check the original. — [The Idea Farm](https://theideafarm.com/markets/extreme-stock-market-performers-part-iv-can-observable-characteristics-forecast-outcomes/); [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=34495)
- Technology stocks appear more often among the worst decade performers than the best. — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=34495)

**Practitioner studies**
- **O'Neil / CAN SLIM.** The Model Book of Greatest Stock Market Winners compiles 1,000+ top winners back to the 1880s; later editions of *How to Make Money in Stocks* cover 1880–2009. Recurring pre-move traits: accelerating EPS and revenue growth, high relative strength, rising volume, new products or management, industry-group leadership, and a breakout from a base to new highs. This is an outcome-first (hindsight) study with no control group of non-winners sharing the traits. — [William O'Neil + Co.](https://www.williamoneil.com/what-we-do); [AAII CAN SLIM article](https://www.aaii.com/files/journal/pdf/9874_william-oneil-can-slim-approach-to-selecting-growth-stocks.pdf)
- **CAN SLIM tests.** Lutey & Rayome (2022, *Journal of Accounting and Finance*) ran a live paper-traded test from July 2014 to February 2017 and beat the S&P 500 by 20%, Nasdaq by 9% and Dow by 17% (cumulative). The window is short and the test is not independently audited. — [JAF](https://mail.articlegateway.com/index.php/JAF/article/view/5134)
- AAII's CAN SLIM screen backtests show about 24.4%/yr (from end-1997) and 23.8%/yr (1998–2007, revised version). These are self-reported backtests. — [AAII](https://www.aaii.com/journal/article/a-can-slim-screen-with-no-float-but-plenty-of-lift); [AAII revised](https://www.aaii.com/journal/article/investing-in-proven-growth-using-can-slim-revised)
- A Taiwanese thesis found few stocks fully meet CAN SLIM and passers did not reliably beat sector indices. A critic notes IBD's claimed 20.7%/yr has no audited record. — [NCCU thesis](https://ah.lib.nccu.edu.tw/handle/140.119/33992); [Liberated Stock Trader](https://liberatedstocktrader.com/?p=4574)
- **Minervini Trend Template / SEPA.** As codified by third parties, the template is: price above the 50/150/200-day MAs; 50 > 150 > 200; a rising 200-day MA; within 25% of the 52-week high; at least 30% above the 52-week low. I found no independent or peer-reviewed backtest. Available "results" are unverified TradingView script claims. — [TradingView trend-template scripts](https://www.tradingview.com/scripts/trend-template/)
- **Mayer, *100 Baggers* (2015).** 365 US stocks that returned 100x between 1962 and 2014 (starting market cap above $50M). The average time to 100x was about 26 years. Holders had to survive multiple large drawdowns. Mayer's main conclusion is that high ROIC sustained over a long reinvestment runway matters most (e.g. 25%/yr for about 20–25 years ≈ 100x). Secondary summaries also cite founder/owner-operators and reasonable entry valuations. Sources conflict on the fastest 100-bagger time (about 5 vs about 16 years). The book updates Thomas Phelps's 1972 *100 to 1 in the Stock Market* (1932–1971). — [Livewire summary](https://www.livewiremarkets.com/wires/100-baggers-common-traits); [Market Folly interview](https://www.marketfolly.com/2018/09/chris-mayer-interview-world-according.html)

### Inferences
- Overlapping evidence (Chan-Jegadeesh-Lakonishok, George-Hwang, O'Neil's descriptive study, Minervini's template) points to the same observable state: a stock near its 52-week high, with strong 6–12 month relative strength, positive earnings surprise or upward revisions, and abnormally high volume. The academic support is for *modest average excess returns over 1–12 months*, not for picking multi-baggers.
- Long-horizon fundamentals (high, sustained ROIC/gross profitability, reinvestment, R&D, youth) are the only features linked to decade-scale winners. Their predictive R² is tiny (about 2%), so they work as weak priors at best.
- PEAD and earnings-gap drift strategies in liquid (non-microcap) US stocks should be assumed largely arbitraged away post-2006. Where it survives, the edge is concentrated in hard-to-trade microcaps.
- The practitioner studies (O'Neil, Minervini, Mayer, Phelps) all condition on the outcome. They describe the traits winners shared but give no base rate of how many stocks with those traits failed, so they cannot establish predictive value alone.

### Gaps
- I could not access exact long-short spreads from the original Jegadeesh-Titman (commonly cited as about 1%/month), Novy-Marx, or Gervais et al. PDFs. The figures above come from secondary summaries.
- I found no independent, peer-reviewed test of Minervini's SEPA/Trend Template.
- I did not find a formal study of the large-cap analyst-revision effect after 2010.
- I did not verify small-cap and value effect sizes or their post-2010 performance in this session. The commonly cited weakness of value in the 2010s and the size effect concentrating in microcaps and January need primary sourcing (e.g. Fama-French data library, AQR "Size Matters, If You Control Your Junk").

## Q2. What are the base rates (e.g., % of stocks that 5x or 10x; fraction of signal hits that succeed)?

### Takeaway
Base rates are brutal. Most individual US stocks fail to beat T-bills over their lifetimes. Roughly 4% of stocks account for all net US market wealth creation since 1926, and 83 firms (0.3%) produced half of it. Ten-baggers within a decade appear to be on the order of 1–2% of stocks in recent Russell 3000 data. I found no well-sourced 5-year 5x/10x base rate. Signal-level hit rates for "multi-bagger" outcomes are not reported in the literature.

### Cited Findings
- Bessembinder, "Do Stocks Outperform Treasury Bills?" JFE 2018 (DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2018.06.004). The majority of CRSP common stocks since 1926 have lifetime buy-and-hold returns below one-month T-bills. The 2017 working-paper wording is "slightly more than four out of every seven." The best-performing 4% of companies explain the net gain of the entire US market since 1926; the rest collectively matched T-bills. — [ASU W.P. Carey summary](https://news.wpcarey.asu.edu/20180917-do-stocks-outperform-treasury-bills); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/treasury-bills-outperform-stocks-say/)
- In the 1926–2016 data, $35 trillion of wealth above T-bills came from 1,092 firms (4.3% of about 26,000). More than half came from just 90 firms. — [ASU W.P. Carey](https://news.wpcarey.asu.edu/20180917-do-stocks-outperform-treasury-bills)
- In the 1926–2019 update, 83 firms (0.32%) generated 50% of $47 trillion in excess wealth, and 5 firms (0.02%) generated 11.9%. — [The Idea Farm](https://theideafarm.com/markets/extreme-stock-market-performers-part-iv-can-observable-characteristics-forecast-outcomes/)
- J.P. Morgan Asset Management studied Russell 3000 stocks over 1980–2020: 42% had negative absolute lifetime returns, and 66% underperformed the index (as reported in a secondary summary). — [Creative Planning summary](https://creativeplanning.com/insights/investment/insights-russell-3000/)
- 10x frequency: about 22 Russell 3000 stocks returned 10x over 2016–2025, roughly 0.7%. The source estimates the 10-year 10x probability at 0.7–2%. This is a secondary blog, likely with survivorship and definitional issues. — [QuantifiedStrategies](https://www.quantifiedstrategies.com/whats-the-probability-of-a-10x-stock/)
- 100-baggers: 365 US stocks over 1962–2014 (starting cap above $50M), taking about 26 years on average. — [Livewire](https://www.livewiremarkets.com/wires/100-baggers-common-traits)
- Top decade performers had suffered prior-decade drawdowns of about 81% on average. Big winners routinely pass through near-wipeouts. — [The Idea Farm](https://theideafarm.com/markets/extreme-stock-market-performers-part-iv-can-observable-characteristics-forecast-outcomes/)
- Mauboussin's *Base Rate Book* (Credit Suisse 2016) and Mauboussin & Callahan's "Drawdowns & Recoveries: Base Rates for Bottoms and Bounces" (Morgan Stanley/Counterpoint) are the relevant base-rate compendia. I could not extract specific "% of stocks rising X% in N years" figures from them in this session. — [Validea on Mauboussin base rates](https://blog.validea.com/michael-mauboussin-on-valuation-base-rates-and-expectations-investing/); [Alpha Architect, Callahan tag](https://alphaarchitect.com/tag/dan-callahan/)

### Inferences
- If roughly 1–2% of stocks 10x over a decade, a 5-year 10x is rarer still, plausibly well under 1% per 5-year window, though this is not directly sourced. Any screen claiming to find multi-baggers should be judged against that prior. Even a 3–5x lift in hit rate leaves most selected names failing.
- Return distributions are extremely positively skewed, so a randomly chosen single stock has a median outcome below T-bills while the index does well. Concentrated portfolios have a high probability of underperforming even if their expected value is fine.

### Gaps
- I found no primary source for a 5-year 5x or 10x base rate in CRSP including delisted firms. This would need direct CRSP computation or the Mauboussin/Callahan reports.
- I found no study reporting the fraction of momentum, 52-week-high or CAN SLIM "signals" that go on to become multi-baggers. The literature reports portfolio means, not right-tail hit rates.

## Q3. How should a systematic trader combine signals and manage risk given fat-tailed outcomes?

### Takeaway
Combine low-correlated, independently supported signals: price momentum or 52-week-high proximity, earnings momentum (EAR/SUE/revisions), profitability/quality, and possibly value as a hedge. Expect crash risk in momentum after market panics, and expect signal decay after publication of about 50%. Because wealth creation is concentrated in a few extreme winners that endure deep drawdowns, the risk framework has to keep the right tail open (let winners run, keep breadth) while capping losses. That combination calls for diversification across many positions rather than heavy concentration.

### Cited Findings
- Momentum crashes, from Daniel & Moskowitz, "Momentum Crashes" (JFE). In July–August 1932 the past-loser decile returned +232% vs +32% for winners. In March–May 2009 losers returned +163% vs +8% for winners. Crashes occur in "panic" states (after market declines, high volatility) and coincide with sharp rebounds, because loser betas rise above 3 and winner betas fall below 0.5. Crashes are partly forecastable, and dynamic or volatility-scaled momentum mitigates them. — [Daniel & Moskowitz NBER w20439](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20439/w20439.pdf); [Chicago Booth Review](https://www.chicagobooth.edu/review/understanding-momentum-crashes); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/avoiding-momentum-crashes/)
- Combining momentum with value reduces drawdowns, because the two are negatively correlated. — [AQR, Fact Fiction and Momentum](https://www.aqr.com/-/media/AQR/Documents/Journal-Articles/JPM-Fact-Fiction-and-Momentum-Investing.pdf)
- Profitability hedges value and raises value's performance when the two are combined. — [NBER w15940](https://nber.org/papers/w15940)
- Price and earnings momentum each carry independent information in Chan-Jegadeesh-Lakonishok (1996). Novy-Marx (2015) argues earnings momentum subsumes price momentum. Either way, earnings-based signals belong in the combination. — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v51y1996i5p1681-1713.html); [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/quantitative-momentum-research-price-and-earnings-momentum/)
- Post-publication decay: McLean & Pontiff (2016) find post-publication anomaly returns about 50% smaller than in-sample (about 25% lower 3 years post-sample and about 40% lower 5 years post-sample). — [Publication Bias in Asset Pricing Research (arXiv 2209.13623)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.13623)
- Hou, Xue & Zhang, "Replicating Anomalies": 64% of about 450 anomalies are insignificant once microcaps are controlled for. Critics dispute the anomaly list. — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/replicating-anomalies/); [arXiv 2209.13623](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2209.13623)
- Momentum strategies have high turnover, so implementation must manage trading costs. — [CFA Institute FAJ 1999](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/1999/the-profitability-of-momentum-strategies)
- Bessembinder argues that the skewness of individual-stock returns explains why poorly diversified active strategies most often underperform. — [ASU W.P. Carey](https://news.wpcarey.asu.edu/20180917-do-stocks-outperform-treasury-bills)
- 100-baggers and top-decade winners typically endured very deep drawdowns (about 81% prior-decade drawdowns for top-decade performers). Tight fixed stops would have exited many eventual big winners, a direct tension with "cut losses short" rules. — [The Idea Farm](https://theideafarm.com/markets/extreme-stock-market-performers-part-iv-can-observable-characteristics-forecast-outcomes/); [Livewire on Mayer](https://www.livewiremarkets.com/wires/100-baggers-common-traits)

### Inferences
- **Signal stack.** A defensible composite ranks on 12-1 month momentum and/or 52-week-high proximity, recent earnings-announcement return or SUE, and upward analyst revisions. It filters on gross profitability and avoids high-leverage, negative-income, high-volatility names (Bessembinder's loser traits). An abnormal-volume trigger can serve as a timing or attention confirmation. Assume each component's live edge is about half its published backtest.
- **Regime overlay.** Scale momentum exposure down after large market drawdowns and high volatility (Daniel-Moskowitz panic states), or volatility-target the book.
- **Sizing.** Given extreme skew and low R², use many small, roughly equal or volatility-scaled positions rather than concentrated bets. The goal is to have exposure to the rare winner. Pyramiding into positions that keep working ("let winners run") fits the momentum evidence that winners do not quickly reverse.
- **Stops.** Practitioner rules (O'Neil and Minervini popularised tight stops in the high single digits) protect against the common failure case but will exit many eventual long-horizon multi-baggers. A trader should choose explicitly between a short-horizon momentum-swing style (tight stops, high turnover, edge in the mean) and a long-horizon compounder style (wide or no price stops, fundamental exits, tolerance for 50–80% drawdowns). Mixing the two rulesets is incoherent.
- **Robustness.** Validate on post-2010 data, exclude microcaps or model their costs realistically, and include delisted stocks to avoid survivorship bias. The O'Neil, Minervini and Mayer studies are hindsight samples and should not be treated as backtests.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve a primary source in this session for O'Neil's 7–8% stop rule or Minervini's specific stop and position-sizing rules. These are widely attributed to their books but are unverified here.
- I found no academic study directly testing trailing stops vs buy-and-hold on momentum or multi-bagger candidate portfolios. Kaminski & Lo's "When do stop-loss rules stop losses?" would be a relevant source but was not retrieved.
- Volatility-managed momentum (e.g. Barroso & Santa-Clara 2015, "Momentum has its moments") is relevant to sizing but was not retrieved in this session.
